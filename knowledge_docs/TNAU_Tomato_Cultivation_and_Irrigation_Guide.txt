# TITLE: Production Technology and Water Management in Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)

## SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Tomato is one of the most widely cultivated commercial vegetable crops in South India, especially in the Coimbatore, Dharmapuri, Krishnagiri, and Dindigul districts of Tamil Nadu.
Optimal Soils:
- Well-drained sandy loam and red loam soils rich in organic matter.
- Soil pH range: 6.0 to 7.0 is ideal. Soils with pH below 5.5 require liming.
- Drainage: Highly sensitive to water stagnation. Stagnant moisture exceeding 24 hours causes oxygen deprivation, root asphyxiation, and collar rot.

## SECTION 2: IRRIGATION REQUIREMENTS AND SOIL MOISTURE THRESHOLDS
Tomato requires precise soil moisture regulation across its growth stages:
- Available Soil Moisture (ASM): Recommended available soil moisture should be maintained between 60% and 70% of field capacity.
- Sensor Reading Guidance:
  - Critical Deficit: Soil moisture below 30% indicates severe water deficit. Prolonged exposure causes blossom-end rot (calcium translocation blockage) and severe flower abortion. Immediate irrigation is necessary.
  - Sub-optimal Deficit: Soil moisture between 30% and 44% warrants prompt irrigation, particularly if the crop is in flowering or fruiting stages.
  - Optimal Range: Soil moisture between 45% and 65% represents optimal root aeration and transpiration. Maintain standard irrigation schedules.
  - Excessive Saturation: Soil moisture above 75% for extended periods restricts root aeration, promotes Pythium and Rhizoctonia root rot, and creates favorable microclimates for fungal blights.

Irrigation Scheduling:
- Drip Irrigation: Recommended discharge is 2 to 3 liters per plant per day during vegetative stage, increasing to 4 to 5 liters per plant per day during peak fruit enlargement.
- Surface/Furrow Irrigation: Irrigate immediately after transplanting, followed by life irrigation on the 3rd day, and subsequent irrigations at intervals of 5 to 7 days depending on soil texture.

## SECTION 3: CLIMATIC PARAMETERS AND TEMPERATURE MANAGEMENT
- Germination Temperature: 20°C to 26°C.
- Favorable Vegetative Growth: Daytime temperatures between 24°C and 30°C, nighttime temperatures between 18°C and 22°C.
- Heat Stress Threshold: Ambient temperatures exceeding 35°C severely impede pollen viability, reduce fruit set percentage, and trigger flower drop. Under heat stress above 35°C, farmers are advised to use 35% agro-shade nets or operate micro-sprinklers for 10 minutes at 1:00 PM to reduce canopy temperature.
- Cold Stress Threshold: Temperatures below 12°C inhibit lycopene synthesis, resulting in mottled yellow-orange coloration.

## SECTION 4: INTEGRATED NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT (INM)
For Hybrid Cultivars (Target Yield: 60-80 t/ha):
- Recommended Dose of Fertilizers (RDF): 150:100:100 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare.
- Basal Application: Apply 50% of Nitrogen (75 kg), entire Phosphorus (100 kg), and 50% of Potassium (50 kg) along with 25 tonnes of well-decomposed Farmyard Manure (FYM) per hectare.
- Top Dressing: Apply remaining 50% Nitrogen and Potassium in two equal split doses at 30 and 45 days after transplanting (DAT).
- Fertigation Schedule: In drip-fertigated systems, apply water-soluble 19:19:19 and Calcium Nitrate weekly.

## SECTION 5: PEST AND DISEASE MANAGEMENT
1. Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV):
   - Vector: Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci).
   - Symptoms: Upward curling of leaves, vein clearing, stunted growth.
   - Management: Install yellow sticky traps (12 per acre). Spray Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.3 ml/L or Neem oil (10,000 ppm) @ 2 ml/L at first appearance of whiteflies.
2. Early Blight (Alternaria solani):
   - Symptoms: Concentric circular dark brown spots on older leaves ("target-board" appearance).
   - Management: Foliar spray with Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2 g/L or Azoxystrobin @ 1 ml/L.
3. Fruit Borer (Helicoverpa armigera):
   - Management: Set up pheromone traps @ 5 per acre. Spray Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) @ 2 g/L or Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC @ 0.3 ml/L.
